# Designing a Domain Design System

**Status:** Exploratory
**Purpose:** A short entry point for exploring what a System that helps people design Domains should be.

## Core Idea

Explore a System that helps people make a Domain understandable and explicit: its purpose and context, Problems and Goals, Terms and Types, relationships, rules, and evidence. The current work is about **what the System should help people express**, not how to implement it.

This is a possible direction for DevEnv, not a claim about current application capabilities or a commitment to build the proposed model.

## Guiding Principles

- Start with purpose, context, and real Problems—not a predetermined schema or solution.
- Distinguish observations, Problem framings, cause hypotheses, Goals, and proposed responses.
- Make Terms and Types meaningful in context; structure alone does not capture meaning.
- Use abstraction to reveal essentials while preserving important differences and traceability to examples.
- Treat validation as layered: structural constraints, domain rules, and evidence-based judgments are different.
- Keep uncertainty, disagreement, and unresolved decisions visible.
- Do not equate a complete or internally consistent model with a useful one.
- Do not mistake a list of features for a definition or structure of the System; connect proposed capabilities to Problems, outcomes, and coherent responsibilities.
- Explore **what** now; defer implementation choices, including TDD and technology, to a later phase.
